How the noise could reach the touchpad

1

At light load, many chargers stop switching steadily and work in bursts. That makes electrical noise at lower frequencies.
A common power-saving trick in USB-C chargers. Not measured on yours.

2

The charger isn't earthed, so that noise rides on the laptop's own ground, which wobbles slightly against the room.

3

Your body sits close to the room's ground. Touching the pad, your finger bridges the two and injects the noise right where you touch.

4

The pad senses tiny charge changes many times a second. If the noise lands near the frequency it scans at, the firmware can't separate noise from finger.

5

Result: ghost second fingers (phantom scrolls and taps), lost touches (jumps), worst where your finger spends the most time, in the middle.
