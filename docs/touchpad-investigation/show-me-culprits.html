What's proven

  • There are two separate faults. A dead band that drifts across the pad (your finger drops and the pointer jumps), and ghost-finger spells (phantom clicks and scrolls).

Top 3 culprits

1

ELAN touchpad firmware (affects the whole model)

Most likely

The pad's own firmware handles noise and its "no touch" baseline badly, so it misreads where your finger is.

For

  • The data is wrong before Linux sees it
  • The dead band moves around, even with no reset, so it's not one broken spot
  • Your other 840 G10 did the same thing
  • Linux is offered only firmware 0x0008, with no updates
  • No bulge, drop or spill

Against

  • Nobody has shown that newer firmware, or Windows, behaves better

Fixes

  • Now: when ghosts appear, press Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F12. It waits until nothing touches the pad, then recalibrates.
  • Permanent: ask HP support for 840 G10 ClickPad firmware newer than 0x0008. HP ships ELAN firmware 22.50.0.0 for the 630/640/650 G10. Don't flash another model's firmware.
  • Fallback: a filter between the pad and the desktop that joins up split-second drops and throws away ghost touches. It's designed but not built.
  • Check: a 90-second recording on your other laptop would confirm whether the whole model is affected.
2

Charger noise (mostly when plugged in and full)

Likely trigger for ghost spells

Electrical noise from the charger reaches the sensor through your finger. The pad sees fake touches and loses real ones.

For

  • You notice that unplugging helps
  • Tonight's spell and the Nov 5 spells all started while plugged in and full
  • The dead band moved around the time you unplugged

Against

  • You're plugged in about 99% of the time, so "it happened while plugged in" proves little
  • Tonight's plug-in test showed no change, but the spell had already faded
  • The dead band also appears on battery

Fixes

  • When it acts up: unplug and keep using the pad for about 5 minutes. If it's still bad, press F12.
  • Cheap try: if the charger cord has a 3-pin plug, use a properly earthed socket.
  • Avoid: a battery charge limit. It keeps the laptop "plugged in, not charging" for longer, which is the state under suspicion.
  • Check: the new log compares before and after every unplug. A few unplugs during a spell will settle it.
3

Heat or waking from sleep

Worth watching

The pad's baseline drifts over time. Something that changes over minutes, like temperature or the power-up after sleep, could be pushing it.

For

  • The dead band moved within 7 minutes with no reset
  • Tonight's bad spell followed waking from sleep at 21:55
  • On resume the pad only gets a "power on" command, never a full reset

Against

  • There's no direct evidence yet. Temperature wasn't recorded before tonight

Fix

  • Check: the log now records CPU temperature and every wake-up. If spells follow either one, an automatic hands-off recalibration after wake-up is the fix.

Ruled out: the acpi_osi boot setting, because the trackpad problems started before it was added.

When it acts up

  1. Unplug and keep using the pad for about 5 minutes. The log captures before and after on its own.
  2. Still bad? Press Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F12, then lift your fingers when asked. It saves the raw data and recalibrates.
  3. Don't press F12 before unplugging, or we can't tell which one fixed it.
